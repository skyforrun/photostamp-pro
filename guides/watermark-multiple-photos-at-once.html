Watermark multiple photos at once, safely

Watermarking a batch is mechanically easy and easy to get wrong. Two mistakes account for nearly every disaster: stamping the master files, and letting the mark move with the crop.

Preview files get stolen more often than finals. A tiled, low-opacity mark on previews is the cheapest insurance you can add.

1. Work on copies, always

Create an output folder before you start. Every method below writes new files and leaves the source untouched — build that into the workflow rather than trusting yourself to be careful.

2. Decide the mark before the batch

Corner mark, diagonal tile, or a date stamp? Tiled marks survive cropping and re-posting better; corner marks are less intrusive on gallery previews. Decide once, then apply it uniformly.

3. Convert the logo to a smart object

A rasterised logo degrades on every resize and cannot be swapped later. Place the logo, convert it to a smart object, and scale from inside that object.

4. Stamp last

If the image will be cropped or resized for delivery, stamp after that step. A watermark placed before a crop moves with the crop and often ends up half off-frame.

5. Keep a log

When a batch runs 500 files, one silent failure among them is invisible. Any scripted route should write a log of what succeeded and what did not.

Questions

Does JPEG compression affect the watermark?

Yes — a light mark can smear at low quality. Save previews at quality 8 or higher.

Can I use different marks for different clients?

With an action you re-record. With a script you save the settings as a preset and switch.

What about PNG with transparency?

Use a transparent PNG for the logo, not a JPG; a white box around the mark is the most common giveaway of an amateur stamp.
