Protecting client photos before sending previews

Watermarks deter casual reuse; they do not prevent theft. What they do buy you is a clear signal of ownership and a paper trail if an image turns up somewhere it should not.

A watermark on a preview is not DRM. It raises the cost of misuse from "free" to "visible", which is usually enough.

1. Tile, do not corner-mark

A single corner mark is cropped off in one gesture. A low-opacity diagonal pattern survives cropping, resizing, and screenshotting.

2. Downscale to what the client needs

A 1600px preview is enough to judge a photo and not enough to print. Do not send full-resolution files for approval.

3. Strip or keep metadata deliberately

GPS and camera serial numbers in delivered previews are a privacy issue. Decide per client whether to strip.

4. Keep the delivered filenames traceable

Include a client or job code. If a file leaks, the code identifies which delivery it came from.

5. Log what you sent

Date, file count, mark style. If a dispute happens six months later, that log is the evidence.

Questions

What opacity works for a tile?

Around 20-35%. Higher ruins the preview, lower disappears after compression.

Should the mark cover faces?

No — clients reject previews where the mark obscures the subject. Tile behind, not across, faces where possible.

Is a logo enough, or do I need a copyright line?

A logo identifies you; a small copyright line identifies the year and the claim. Both, on previews.
